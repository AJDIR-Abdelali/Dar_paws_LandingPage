Fleas and ticks are more than an annoyance: they can transmit **serious diseases** to your pet, and sometimes to people. In Morocco, the mild climate allows them to be active much of the year. Here's how to protect your companion.

## Fleas: how to spot them

- your pet **scratches** a lot, especially at the base of the tail;
- you see **small black dots** in its coat (flea droppings): on damp white paper, they turn reddish;
- **red spots** or hair loss;
- you get bitten yourself, on the ankles.

Fleas lay eggs **in the environment**: baskets, carpets, sofas. Treating the animal alone is often not enough.

## Ticks: why they're dangerous

Ticks attach to the skin to feed on blood. They can transmit diseases such as **babesiosis (piroplasmosis)** or **ehrlichiosis** in dogs, which can be serious.

After each walk in grass, scrubland or the countryside, **check your dog**: ears, neck, armpits, between the toes.

## How to remove a tick

1. Use a **tick remover** (sold at vets and pharmacies).
2. Slide it under the tick, as close to the skin as possible.
3. **Turn gently** without pulling, until the tick comes off.
4. Disinfect the area and wash your hands.

> Never use ether, alcohol or a lighter on a tick: it can make it regurgitate and increase the risk of infection.

## Available treatments

| Type | How it works |
|---|---|
| Spot-on pipettes | applied to the skin, usually every month |
| Tablets | given by mouth, protection of one to three months depending on the product |
| Collars | protection of several months |
| Sprays and shampoos | as a complement |

Your vet chooses the right product according to the animal's weight, age and lifestyle.

## ⚠️ The dangerous mistake with cats

> **Never put a dog product on a cat.** Some dog pipettes contain **permethrin**, which is very toxic for cats and can cause trembling, seizures and death.

Always use a product **made for the species and the weight** of your pet. If your cat lives with a treated dog, ask your vet's advice.

## Also treat the house

- **Wash baskets and blankets** at high temperature;
- **vacuum** carefully (and throw away the bag);
- use an environment product if advised by the vet.

## And worms?

Intestinal worms are another problem, often linked to fleas (some worms are transmitted by swallowing a flea). **Deworming** every three months for adults, more often for puppies and kittens, according to your vet.

## Which schedule?

- **Fleas and ticks**: depending on the product, every month to every three months, all year round or at least in the warm season.
- **Deworming**: often every three months for adults.

## Risk areas in Morocco

Ticks and fleas are almost everywhere, but some places need more attention:

- **the countryside and forests**: for example on outings to the Maâmora forest or in the mountains;
- **wasteland and tall grass** in town;
- **gardens** with lots of vegetation;
- **places where stray dogs live**, which often carry parasites;
- **boarding facilities** where many animals mix.

**Spring and summer** are the most active seasons, but with Morocco's mild climate, ticks are found for much of the year. That's why many vets recommend protection **all year round**.

If you travel with your dog, or leave it with a sitter, check its treatment is **up to date before you leave**. And ask the sitter to inspect its coat after every walk.

## FAQ

**My cat never goes out, does it need a treatment?**
Fleas can come in on your clothes or with another animal. Ask your vet about a suitable schedule.

**Are natural remedies effective?**
Some products can repel, but their effectiveness is often limited. And some essential oils are toxic for cats.

**Can I treat a puppy?**
Yes, with a product suitable for its age and weight, on your vet's advice.
