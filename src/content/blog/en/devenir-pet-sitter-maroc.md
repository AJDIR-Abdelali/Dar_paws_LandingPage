More and more Moroccan families have a dog or cat, and they need someone to look after them during work, celebrations or holidays. If you love animals, **becoming a pet sitter** can be a pleasant and flexible extra income. Here's how to start.

## Is this job for you?

Pet sitting is more than cuddles. Ask yourself:

- Am I **reliable and punctual**? An animal waiting for its meal can't wait.
- Can I handle **unexpected events** calmly (illness, escape, damage)?
- Do I have **time** at the times owners need it (mornings, evenings, holidays)?
- Is my **home** suitable if I offer boarding?

## Services you can offer

| Service | For whom |
|---|---|
| **Home visits** | cats, small animals, during holidays |
| **Dog walking** | owners who work late |
| **Hourly sitting** | an evening, a day |
| **Boarding at your home** | for stays of several days |
| **Grooming** | if you have training |
| **Training & behaviour** | if you're a trained trainer |

Start with what you master, then expand.

## Useful skills

- know the **signs of a sick animal** and react quickly;
- know the **basics of dog behaviour** (stress, calming signals);
- know how to **give medication**;
- know the **dangers**: toxic foods, heat, balconies;
- **first aid** training is a real plus.

## How to set your prices

There's no official rate. To decide:

1. **Look at prices** around you, in your neighbourhood and city;
2. take into account your **time and travel**;
3. add a supplement for **special needs** (medication, several animals, holidays);
4. stay **clear and transparent**: price per hour, per walk, per night or per session.

## Build trust

Owners entrust you with what they love most. To reassure them:

- a **complete profile** with a real photo and a sincere presentation;
- **identity verification** when the platform offers it;
- a **meeting before** each new sitting;
- **regular news** with photos;
- **reviews** from satisfied clients, which become your best advertising.

## Find your first clients

- Start with **friends, family and neighbours**.
- Ask for a **review** after each sitting.
- Be **available during holidays**: that's when demand peaks.
- Use an app that shows your profile to owners **near you**.

## Organise yourself well

- Keep a **sheet per animal**: habits, food, vet, emergency contact.
- Don't accept **more animals** than you can handle.
- Agree on terms **before** the sitting: dates, price, services.
- Have a **backup plan** if you're ill.

## What owners really expect

Owners aren't only looking for someone who loves animals. Above all, they want to be **reassured**. What makes the difference:

- **punctuality**: arriving at the promised time, every visit;
- **regular news**: one photo and a short message a day is enough;
- **following instructions**: food, times, house rules;
- **discretion**: respecting the home and privacy;
- **honesty**: telling them straight away about any problem, even a small one.

A happy owner comes back, leaves a good review and recommends you to friends. In Morocco, **word of mouth** is often the first source of clients.

Protect yourself too: write down the agreed **dates, prices and services**, keep a record of your messages, and never take an animal you don't feel able to care for.

## FAQ

**Do I need a diploma?**
To start pet sitting, no diploma is required. For grooming or training, training is strongly recommended.

**Can I be a sitter as a student?**
Yes, it's a common flexible job, as long as you're reliable.

**How do I handle an aggressive animal?**
Never take risks. Talk with the owner beforehand, and refuse a sitting if you don't feel able.
