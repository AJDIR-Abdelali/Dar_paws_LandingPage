In Morocco, summer temperatures often exceed 35 °C, and sometimes much more in Marrakech, Fès or the south. Dogs and cats don't sweat like us: they regulate heat mainly by **panting**. When it's too hot, their temperature can rise dangerously: this is **heatstroke**, a real emergency.

## Animals most at risk

- **Flat-faced breeds**: French bulldogs, pugs, Persian cats, shih tzus…
- **Puppies and kittens**, **senior** animals;
- **Overweight** animals or those with heart or breathing problems;
- **Thick-coated** dogs (husky, malinois in full sun);
- very **energetic** dogs that don't know when to stop.

## The danger of a parked car

> Never leave your pet in a car, even "just five minutes", even with the windows ajar. The inside temperature rises extremely fast in the sun.

## Signs of heatstroke

At the start:

- **very heavy, noisy panting**;
- heavy drooling;
- very red gums and tongue;
- agitation, looking for shade.

Then, more serious:

- staggering, weakness;
- vomiting, diarrhoea;
- pale or bluish gums;
- confusion, collapse, seizures.

## First aid

1. **Move the animal into shade** or a cool room straight away.
2. **Wet it with cool water**, not ice-cold (ice water constricts blood vessels): especially the belly, paws, neck.
3. **Ventilate**: fan, air conditioning, open window.
4. **Offer water** in small amounts, without forcing.
5. **Go to the vet quickly**, even if it seems to recover. Some damage appears later.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Shade and cool water | Ice cubes or ice water |
| Ventilate | Cover with a wet towel that heats up |
| Small sips of water | Force it to drink |
| Call the vet | Wait to see if it passes |

## Preventing heatstroke in summer

- **Walk at cool times**: early morning and evening.
- **Test the ground with your hand**: if the asphalt burns your skin after 5 seconds, it burns their paws.
- **Fresh water always available**, in several places.
- **A cool place** in the house: tiles, shade, fan.
- **Brush regularly** to remove dead hair. Don't shave certain breeds without advice: the coat also protects from the sun.
- **Avoid intense exercise** during heat waves.
- **Cooling mat** or damp towel in the shade.

## Cats in summer

Cats tolerate heat better, but are not immune:

- make sure they have **shaded spots**;
- watch **balconies and verandas** exposed to the sun;
- put several **water bowls** around, or a fountain.

## When you travel in summer

If you leave your pet with a sitter in summer, explain your **walk times** and the importance of water and shade. A good sitter knows these rules, but saying them reassures everyone.

## A typical summer day with a dog

Here's a simple routine to protect your dog during hot spells:

- **6 to 8 a.m.**: the real walk of the day, with play and sniffing.
- **10 a.m. to 5 p.m.**: rest indoors, somewhere cool. Only short outings for toilet breaks, in the shade.
- **All day**: fresh water refilled often, several bowls, a tiled corner or a cooling mat.
- **After 7 p.m.**: second walk, once the ground has cooled.

To keep your dog busy during the hot hours, offer **calm games**: snuffle mats, toys filled with treats, short training exercises. You can also freeze a little unsalted broth inside a toy for it to lick in the cool.

If you go to the beach, choose the cool hours, bring shade and fresh water, and never let your dog run for long on burning sand.

## FAQ

**What temperature is dangerous for a dog?**
It depends on the breed, humidity and activity. From 30 °C, be very careful, especially with flat-faced breeds.

**Can I put my dog in the sea to cool it?**
Yes, if it likes water, but rinse it with fresh water afterwards and don't let it drink sea water.

**Should I shave my dog in summer?**
Not always. Ask a groomer or vet: for some breeds, the coat protects from heat.
