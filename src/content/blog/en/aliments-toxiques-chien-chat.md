Some foods we eat every day are **dangerous, even deadly**, for dogs and cats. During celebrations and family meals, the risk increases: a piece of cake, a bit of tagine, leftovers given under the table… Here's the list to know.

## The most dangerous foods

| Food | Danger |
|---|---|
| **Chocolate** (especially dark) | theobromine: vomiting, agitation, heart problems |
| **Grapes and raisins** | kidney failure, even in small amounts in dogs |
| **Onion, garlic, leek** | destroy red blood cells (raw, cooked or powdered) |
| **Xylitol** (sweetener) | in chewing gum, sweets: hypoglycaemia, liver damage |
| **Avocado** | risky for some animals, especially birds and rodents |
| **Cooked bones** | splinter and can pierce the intestine |
| **Alcohol and coffee** | very toxic even in small amounts |
| **Macadamia nuts** | weakness, trembling in dogs |
| **Raw dough** (with yeast) | swells in the stomach |

Source and full list: [ASPCA Animal Poison Control](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control/people-foods-avoid-feeding-your-pets).

> Onion and garlic are in many Moroccan dishes (tagine, harira, sauces). Never give your pet leftovers seasoned with them.

## Also watch out for

- **Human medication**: paracetamol and ibuprofen are very dangerous, especially for cats.
- **Toxic plants**: lilies (deadly for cats), oleander, some houseplants.
- **Cleaning products** and rat poison.
- **Spoiled food** in the bin.

## Symptoms of poisoning

Depending on the product, signs can appear within minutes or after several hours:

- vomiting, diarrhoea;
- heavy drooling;
- agitation or, on the contrary, great tiredness;
- trembling, seizures;
- difficulty breathing;
- pale gums.

## What to do in an emergency

1. **Remove the product** and note what was eaten, how much and when.
2. **Call a vet straight away**, even if your pet seems fine.
3. **Don't make it vomit** without the vet's advice: it can be dangerous.
4. **Bring the packaging** to the clinic.

The faster you act, the better the chances.

## Safe treats

You can give, in small quantities:

- for a dog: plain carrot, apple without seeds, plain cooked rice, unseasoned chicken;
- for a cat: a little plain cooked chicken or fish, without bones.

Treats should stay **under 10% of daily intake**.

## Celebrations and holidays

During Eid, weddings and family gatherings:

- ask guests **not to feed** your pet;
- keep plates and bins **out of reach**;
- give your pet a **quiet room** if there's a lot of noise.

## Watch out for Moroccan dishes and sweets

Some specialities we love should be strictly avoided for our pets:

- **tagines, harira and sauces**: onion, garlic and spices are toxic or irritating;
- **pastries** (gazelle horns, chebakia, sellou): sugar, honey, lots of almonds, sometimes chocolate;
- **dates**: the stone can block the intestine, and the sugar isn't suitable;
- **mint tea**: theine is bad for animals, and it's very sweet;
- **salted olives and cured meats**: too much salt and fat;
- **lamb bones** during Eid: cooked or raw, they can splinter and cause injury.

During **Eid al-Adha**, be especially careful: leftover meat, bones and offal are often lying around. Don't let your dog dig through rubbish bags.

If you want to treat your pet during the celebration, prepare a small piece of **cooked, plain, boneless** meat. That way the whole family celebrates, safely.

## FAQ

**My dog ate a small piece of milk chocolate, is it serious?**
It depends on the amount and the dog's weight. Call the vet: they will tell you whether to come in.

**Is milk good for cats?**
Most adult cats digest lactose poorly. Water is enough.

**Can my dog eat raw bones?**
Opinions differ. Ask your vet; cooked bones are in any case to be avoided.
