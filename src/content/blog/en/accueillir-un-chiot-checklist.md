A puppy arriving is joy… and a lot of questions. What to buy? How to prepare the house? How to get through the first nights? This checklist helps you get ready **before** the big day and start on the right foot.

## The equipment to plan

| Item | Tip |
|---|---|
| Food and water bowls | stainless steel or ceramic, easy to clean |
| Puppy food | the same as at the breeder/family, at least at first |
| Basket or bed | in a quiet place, not in a corridor |
| Collar and lead | adjustable, it will grow |
| Tag | with your phone number |
| Chew toys | to protect your shoes and furniture |
| Puppy pads | for the first weeks |
| Brush | suited to its coat |
| Carrier or crate | for the car and the vet |

## Prepare the house

Puppies explore everything with their mouth. Before it arrives:

- **hide electrical cables**;
- put **cleaning products and medication** out of reach;
- remove **toxic plants**;
- close off **stairs and balconies**;
- put away **shoes, socks and small objects** it could swallow.

## The first day

1. **Stay calm**: let it discover the house at its own pace.
2. **Show it its spot**: bed, water, food.
3. **Take it out often**: after waking, after meals, after play.
4. **Limit visits** in the first days.

> A puppy sleeps up to 18 hours a day. Let it rest; it's essential for its development.

## The first nights

The first nights can be hard: it misses its mother and siblings.

- Put its bed **near you** at first, then move it away gradually.
- Put an **item that smells** of its old home or of you.
- **Don't punish** it if it cries; reassure it calmly, without overdoing it.

## The first vet visit

In the **first week**:

- general check-up;
- **vaccines** (from 8 weeks, then boosters);
- **deworming** and antiparasitic treatment;
- discussion about the **microchip** and later **neutering**.

## House training

Patience is key:

- take it out **every 2 hours** at first, and after each meal or nap;
- **reward** it right away when it does its business outside;
- clean accidents **without scolding**, with a product that removes the smell (no bleach, which attracts it back);
- most puppies are house-trained within a few months.

## Socialisation: a key period

Between **3 and 12 weeks** (and a bit after), the puppy learns what is "normal". Show it gently:

- different people (children, adults);
- sounds of the city, the car, the street;
- other calm, vaccinated animals.

A well-socialised puppy becomes a balanced, less fearful adult.

## The budget

Plan for food, vaccines, deworming, the first equipment, neutering later, and **sitting** when you travel.

## Common mistakes in the first weeks

Even with the best intentions, some mistakes come up often:

- **Too much freedom too soon**: let it discover the house room by room.
- **Changing food suddenly**: make the switch over a week.
- **Playing with your hands**: it learns to bite. Always use a toy.
- **Leaving it alone too long** from the start: get it used to a few minutes, then more.
- **Punishing toilet accidents**: it doesn't understand and may become fearful.
- **Forgetting to weigh it**: weight shows whether it's growing well and is used to dose treatments.
- **Giving in to everything**: the rules must be the same for the whole family, from day one.

If you work all day, arrange for someone to **take it out at lunchtime** in the first months. A puppy can't hold on for hours, and long periods alone can create anxiety.

## FAQ

**At what age can a puppy be taken home?**
Not before 8 weeks: it learns important things with its mother and siblings.

**Can it go out before its vaccines?**
In calm, clean places, yes, for socialisation. Avoid places with many unknown dogs.

**How many meals a day?**
Often 3 to 4 meals for a young puppy, then 2 as an adult. Follow the food's recommendations and the vet's advice.
