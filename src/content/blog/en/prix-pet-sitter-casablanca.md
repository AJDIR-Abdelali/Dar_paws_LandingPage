A weekend in Marrakech, a long day at work, a wedding in the evening… Every owner sometimes needs someone for their dog or cat. In Casablanca, pet care services are growing fast. But **how much should you budget**, and how do you avoid bad surprises?

## The different types of care

Before talking prices, know what you need:

- **Dog walking**: a sitter takes your dog out for 30 minutes to an hour. Ideal if you work late.
- **Home visits**: they come to feed your cat, clean the litter and play. Cats often prefer to stay home.
- **A few hours of sitting**: for an evening or a day.
- **Boarding**: your pet sleeps at the sitter's home or a professional boarding facility while you're away.
- **Grooming or training**: one-off sessions with a professional.

## What prices in Casablanca?

There's no official price list: **each sitter sets their own prices**. Some benchmarks:

- some **cage-free boarding** places in Casablanca advertise about **100 MAD a day for a cat and 130 MAD a day for a dog**, food often provided by the owner (prices published in the lifestyle press);
- classified ads offer cheaper boarding, but check the conditions carefully;
- for **walks** and **hourly sitting**, prices vary a lot by neighbourhood and experience.

> Example on DarPaws: a sitter may show **50 MAD per hour of sitting, 30 MAD per walk and 150 MAD per night**. These amounts are just an example; every sitter chooses their own.

## What changes the price

- **Duration**: a night costs more than a walk, and a week can sometimes be negotiated.
- **Number of pets**: two dogs take more work.
- **Special needs**: medication, a senior pet, a very energetic dog.
- **Distance** and neighbourhood.
- **Holidays**: during Eid and the summer holidays, demand soars.

## How to pay neither too much nor too little

A very low price can hide a lack of experience or seriousness. A high price should match a real service. To compare:

1. check **what's included** (number of walks, photos, time spent);
2. read **reviews** from other owners;
3. ask for a **meeting before** the first booking;
4. make sure the **terms are clear** before confirming.

## The special case of Casablanca

Casablanca is a big city, and needs change a lot from one neighbourhood to another. In Maârif, Gauthier or Racine, flats are often small and dogs need regular outings. In Californie, Bouskoura or Dar Bouazza, houses with gardens make home sitting or boarding at the sitter's easier.

A few tips specific to the city:

- **traffic**: a sitter who lives far away may arrive late at rush hour. Prefer someone **in your neighbourhood**.
- **the big holidays**: many Casablancans leave at the same time in summer and during Eid. Book early.
- **heat and humidity** in summer: walks should happen early in the morning or in the evening.
- **green spaces**: ask where the sitter walks dogs (park, corniche, quiet street).

With a map of sitters close to home, you save time and avoid unnecessary trips, for you and for them.

And if you have a regular need, such as a daily walk while you're at work, ask about a weekly arrangement: many sitters prefer stable clients and plan their time around them.

## FAQ

**Do I pay in advance?**
The final price and payment are agreed between you and the sitter. Agree clearly before the booking.

**What should I ask at the first meeting?**
Their experience, how they handle an emergency, whether they have other pets, and whether they can send photos.

**How far ahead should I book?**
One to two weeks, and earlier for holidays and summer.
