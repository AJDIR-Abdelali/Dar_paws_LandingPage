You're going away for a few days and your pet can't come. Two big options: a **boarding facility** (kennel or cattery) or a **pet sitter**, who visits your home or welcomes your pet into theirs. Neither is better in every case: it depends on your pet, the length of your trip and your budget.

## Option 1: professional boarding

A boarding facility hosts several animals in a dedicated space, with staff.

**Pros:**

- staff present all day, sometimes a vet on call;
- adapted spaces, play areas for dogs;
- well suited to long stays.

**Cons:**

- an unfamiliar, sometimes noisy place: some pets get stressed;
- contact with other animals (vaccines required);
- availability is limited during holidays.

In Casablanca, some **cage-free boarding** places advertise about **100 MAD a day for a cat and 130 MAD a day for a dog** (prices published in the lifestyle press). Prices vary by city and service.

## Option 2: the pet sitter at your home

The sitter comes to your home once or several times a day: food, water, litter, walk, play.

**Pros:**

- your pet **stays in its territory**, ideal for cats;
- its routine changes little;
- the house looks lived-in.

**Cons:**

- your pet is alone between visits;
- not ideal for a dog that can't bear solitude;
- you give your keys: trust is essential.

## Option 3: boarding at the sitter's home

Your pet goes to live with the sitter for the duration of your trip, often as the only guest or with few others.

**Pros:**

- a family atmosphere, constant presence;
- more personal attention than at a large facility.

**Cons:**

- a new place to discover;
- check the sitter's home is secure (balcony, garden, other animals).

## Quick comparison

| Criterion | Boarding | Sitter at your home | At the sitter's |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stress for a cat | high | low | medium |
| Presence | continuous | a few visits | continuous |
| Anxious dog | medium | not ideal | good |
| Long stay | good | medium | good |

## Which choice for which pet?

- **Cat**: it usually prefers to stay home. Home visits are often the best option.
- **Sociable, energetic dog**: boarding or a sitter's home, with walks and play.
- **Anxious or senior dog**: a sitter's home, in a calm environment.
- **Pet on medication**: choose someone experienced, and leave written instructions.

## Before you leave, in any case

1. Check **vaccines are up to date**.
2. Prepare **food for the whole stay**, plus a little extra.
3. Leave a **sheet** with habits, the vet's contact and yours.
4. Arrange a **trial** (a short stay or visit) before a long trip.
5. Ask the sitter to send **photos**.

## Questions to ask a boarding facility

If you choose boarding, visit **before** booking. A good facility is happy to welcome a visit. Ask:

- Are dogs **in cages, in kennels or free** in shared spaces?
- **How many outings** per day, and how long?
- Who is there **at night**?
- Which **vaccines** are required for the other animals?
- What happens if your pet **falls ill**: which vet, who tells you?
- Can you bring **its food, bed and toy**?

Also look at **cleanliness**, smell, noise and the condition of the animals already there. If they seem calm and well cared for, that's a good sign.

For a cat, ask whether the cattery is **separate from the dogs**: barking stresses cats a lot. And if your pet has never been looked after, test a single night first before leaving for a week.

When you pick your pet up, ask how the stay went: appetite, sleep, behaviour. It helps you choose the same place, or another, next time.

## FAQ

**How much does home sitting cost compared to boarding?**
It depends on the number of visits per day. For a cat with one visit a day, it's often comparable or cheaper.

**Can I leave my cat alone for a weekend?**
Even with plenty of food, it's risky: a spilled water bowl or a health problem can become serious. One visit a day is the minimum.

**What if my pet gets sick while I'm away?**
Leave the sitter your vet's contact and agree in advance how to handle an emergency.
