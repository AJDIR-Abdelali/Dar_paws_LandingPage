"He's always hungry!" Many owners say it… and that's often how pets put on weight. In Morocco as elsewhere, being **overweight** is one of the most common health problems in indoor dogs and cats. Here's how to find the right amount.

## 1. Start with the table on the bag

Every bag of kibble shows a **daily amount by the animal's weight**. It's a starting point, not an exact rule:

- use your pet's **ideal weight** (not its current weight if it's too heavy);
- the amount is **for the whole day**, split into several meals;
- a **very active** pet may need a little more, one that sleeps all day a little less.

> Weigh the kibble with a **kitchen scale** at least once, then mark the level on a cup. "One bowl" can hold twice what your pet needs.

## 2. Look at and feel your pet

This is the best way to know if the amount is right:

- **ribs**: you should feel them easily under your fingers, without seeing them from a distance;
- **waist**: seen from above, there's a slight waist behind the ribs;
- **belly**: seen from the side, it tucks up a little towards the back legs.

If it's too round, **cut by about 10%** and check again in 3 to 4 weeks. If it's too thin, increase a little and talk to your vet.

## 3. How many meals a day?

| Animal | Meals per day |
|---|---|
| Puppy (up to 6 months) | 3 to 4 |
| Adult dog | 2 |
| Kitten | 3 to 4, or free-feeding if your vet agrees |
| Adult cat | several small meals (cats like to nibble) |

For large dogs that eat fast, two meals rather than one and **no intense exercise right after** eating, to avoid stomach problems.

## 4. Treats count too

Treats, bits of bread, cheese, khobz dipped in sauce… it all adds up. Simple rule: treats shouldn't exceed **about 10% of what it eats** in a day. For training, use a few kibbles taken from its daily portion.

And watch out for dangerous foods: chocolate, grapes, onion, garlic, cooked bones… see our article on [toxic foods](/en/blog/aliments-toxiques-chien-chat).

## 5. Neutered cats: watch the weight

After neutering, a cat needs **less energy** but often has **more appetite**. That's when many cats gain weight. Ask your vet whether to switch to "sterilised" kibble and adjust the amount.

## 6. Switch kibble gently

A sudden change often causes diarrhoea. Make the transition over **about 7 days**:

1. days 1–2: 3/4 old + 1/4 new;
2. days 3–4: half and half;
3. days 5–6: 1/4 old + 3/4 new;
4. day 7: 100% new.

## 7. Fresh water, always

A bowl of **clean** water, changed every day. Cats often drink little: a wide bowl, away from the food and the litter box, or a fountain, encourages them. A pet that suddenly **drinks much more** than usual should see the vet.

## What about home-cooked food?

It's possible, but "rice + chicken" every day **isn't enough** in the long run: it lacks vitamins and minerals (especially calcium). If you want to cook for your pet, have a **vet** design the recipe.

## When to ask the vet

- it **loses weight** for no reason, or gains weight on a normal portion;
- it has **lost its appetite** for more than 24 hours (cat) or 48 hours (dog);
- it vomits often or has diarrhoea for several days.
