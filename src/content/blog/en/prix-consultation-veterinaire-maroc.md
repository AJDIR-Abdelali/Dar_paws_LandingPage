Your dog is limping, your cat has stopped eating, or it's simply time for a vaccine booster: the question comes quickly, **how much will the vet cost?** Here are 2026 benchmarks and tips to pay a fair price without neglecting your pet's health.

## How much is a basic consultation?

According to clinic directories published in 2026, a **basic consultation** for a medium-sized dog or cat at a private clinic costs **about 150 to 350 MAD**. The price depends on:

- the **city** (Casablanca and Rabat are often more expensive);
- the **neighbourhood** and the clinic's equipment;
- the **type of animal**: consultations for exotic pets (rabbits, birds, reptiles) often cost a little more.

These figures are **indicative**. The simplest is to call the clinic before going.

## What often comes on top

The consultation is only the base. Depending on the problem, other costs may be added:

| Service | What to expect |
|---|---|
| Vaccine | vaccine price + consultation |
| Blood test | depends on the tests |
| X-ray or ultrasound | depends on the clinic |
| Medication | depends on the treatment |
| Night or weekend emergency | often an extra charge |

> Always ask for an **estimate before tests**. A good vet explains what is essential and what can wait.

## When is it an emergency?

Don't wait, call a vet if your pet:

- struggles to **breathe**;
- **vomits** several times or has blood in its stool;
- has eaten something **toxic** (chocolate, grapes, medication…);
- can't **stand up** or has a seizure;
- has a swollen belly and tries to vomit without success (an emergency in large dogs);
- has had **heatstroke** or an accident.

In these cases, a few hours can matter. Keep the number of a clinic open at night in your phone.

## How to prepare the visit

A well-prepared visit saves time, and sometimes money:

1. **Write down the symptoms**: since when, how often, what changed.
2. **Bring the health record** or the list of vaccines and treatments.
3. **Take a photo** of what worries you (stool, vomit, wound) if useful.
4. **Carry your cat in a closed carrier**, and keep your dog on a lead.
5. **Prepare your questions** so you don't forget anything.

## Saving money safely

- **Prevention costs less than illness**: up-to-date vaccines, regular parasite treatment, good food.
- **A yearly check-up** catches problems early.
- **Compare prices** for planned procedures (neutering, dental cleaning), not in emergencies.
- **Never give human medication** without advice: some are very dangerous for animals.

## Finding a good vet near you

The best time to choose a vet is **before you need one**. A few simple criteria:

- **proximity**: in an emergency, every minute counts;
- **opening hours** and whether they can see you in the evening or at the weekend;
- **equipment**: X-ray, ultrasound, on-site tests;
- **communication**: a good vet explains the diagnosis and options without rushing you;
- **reviews** from other owners in the neighbourhood.

Make a first simple visit, for a vaccine or a check-up. You'll see how the clinic welcomes your pet, and your vet will know its history the day a real problem happens.

Think about **unusual pets** too: rabbits, birds, tortoises and other exotic animals aren't treated by every clinic. Ask in advance whether the vet is used to these species.

Finally, save the number of an **emergency clinic** open at night in your city: in an emergency at 2 a.m., you won't have time to search.

## FAQ

**Can the vet come to my home?**
Some vets offer home visits, often more expensive than at the clinic. Handy for a very stressed or elderly pet.

**Do I have to pay right away?**
Usually yes. For large bills, ask whether you can pay in instalments.

**How often should I go to the vet?**
At least once a year for a healthy adult, more often for puppies, kittens and senior pets.
