The living-room rug, the bed, the laundry… A cat that pees outside its litter box is very frustrating. But a cat **never does it to annoy you**. It's a message: something is wrong, in its body or in its environment.

## First: the emergency not to miss

> A cat (especially a **male**) that keeps going to the litter box, **strains with nothing coming out**, cries while urinating or licks its lower belly a lot may have a **blocked urethra**. This is a **life-threatening emergency**: see a vet **the same day**, don't wait until tomorrow.

## Medical causes

Before thinking about behaviour, check health. The vet will look in particular for:

- **cystitis** (bladder inflammation), very common in cats and often linked to stress;
- urinary **crystals or stones**;
- a **urinary infection**;
- in older cats: **diabetes**, **kidney disease**, **arthritis** (it hurts to step over the edge of the box).

A cat that felt pain while peeing in its box may **associate the box with pain** and go elsewhere, even after it has healed.

## Litter box causes

Cats are very particular. Check:

### Cleanliness
Remove poop and pee **every day**, and change all the litter regularly. Many cats refuse a dirty box.

### Number of boxes
The rule: **one box per cat, plus one**. Two cats = three boxes, in different places.

### Location
- a **quiet** spot, not next to the washing machine or in a corridor;
- **away from its food and water**;
- easy to reach (not up the stairs for an old cat).

### Box and litter
- a box that's **big enough** (about one and a half times the cat's length);
- some cats dislike **covered boxes**, which trap smells;
- a **change of litter** (scent, texture) can be enough to put it off. If you change, do it gradually;
- avoid heavily scented litter.

## Stress

Cats are creatures of habit. Things that can stress them:

- **moving house**, building work, new furniture;
- a new **baby**, a new pet or guests;
- a **cat outside** that it sees through the window;
- conflict with another cat at home (blocking access to the box);
- your absence during holidays.

Give it **hiding places** and **high spots**, keep a steady routine, and play with it every day.

## Marking

Pee **sprayed onto a vertical surface** (wall, curtain, door) isn't an "accident": it's **marking**. It's common in **unneutered** cats, male and female. **Neutering** greatly reduces it, especially when done early. ## Clean it properly

> **Don't use bleach or ammonia products**: their smell resembles urine and draws the cat back to the same spot.

Use an **enzymatic cleaner** (from pet shops or vets), or failing that, hot soapy water followed by diluted white vinegar. Then make the spot less inviting: put its bowl or a toy there, or cover it for a few days.

## What not to do

- **Don't punish it** or rub its nose in it: it will fear you and become even more stressed.
- Don't put the litter box somewhere it was surprised or frightened.

## In short

1. Straining without peeing? **Vet immediately.**
2. Otherwise, a vet visit to rule out a medical cause.
3. Clean litter, enough boxes, quiet spot, away from food.
4. Reduce stress; neuter if it's marking.
5. Clean without bleach.
