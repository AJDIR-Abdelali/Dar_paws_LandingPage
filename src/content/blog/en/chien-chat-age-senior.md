It sleeps more, climbs the stairs more slowly, its muzzle is turning grey… Your companion is getting older. Thanks to better care and food, our pets live longer and longer. Here's how to help yours age **comfortably**.

## When is a pet a "senior"?

It mostly depends on size:

| Animal | Senior from about |
|---|---|
| Cat | 10–11 years |
| Small dog (under 10 kg) | 9–10 years |
| Medium dog (10–25 kg) | 8 years |
| Large dog (over 25 kg) | 6–7 years |

These are guidelines: every animal ages at its own pace.

## Signs to watch for

Many changes are put down to "he's just old" when they can be treated:

- **stiffness**, limping, trouble getting up, jumping or getting into the car: often **arthritis**, which hurts but can be relieved;
- it **drinks and pees much more**: worth checking (kidneys, diabetes…);
- it **loses** or **gains weight** with no change in food;
- **bad breath**, chewing on one side, dropping kibble: a **dental** problem;
- it bumps into things, is scared in the dark, doesn't hear you call;
- it seems **lost**, paces, barks at night, forgets its house training;
- new **lumps** under the skin;
- it coughs or gets out of breath quickly.

> Note what you see (since when, at what moment) and tell your vet. A small change spotted early is often easier to treat.

## Vet check-ups

For a senior pet, many vets recommend a visit **every 6 months** rather than once a year. The check-up may include:

- a full exam (heart, weight, teeth, joints);
- a **blood test** and urine test to check the kidneys, liver and blood sugar;
- for cats, **blood pressure** and thyroid.

Keep up **vaccinations** and **parasite** treatments: an older animal has weaker defences.

## Food

- **Senior** kibble, or food suited to its health, chosen with your vet.
- Watch its **weight**: extra weight strains joints and the heart, while weight loss can hide an illness. Weigh it regularly.
- If chewing is hard, wet food or kibble **softened in a little warm water** helps.
- Always **fresh water** within reach, in several places in the house.

## An adapted home

- A **thick, soft bed**, away from cold and draughts (tiles are hard on old joints).
- **Non-slip rugs** on slippery tiles.
- A **ramp** or small step for the sofa or the car.
- For cats: a **low-sided** litter box, and food it can reach without jumping.
- Don't move the furniture around too much if its eyesight is failing.

## Activity and wellbeing

An older pet still needs to move, but **differently**:

- **shorter, more frequent** walks, at its pace;
- avoid strong heat and severe cold;
- calm games: finding hidden kibble, stuffable toys;
- lots of **cuddles** and company. Gentle brushing is also a good moment to check its skin.

## When to see the vet quickly

- it hasn't eaten for more than a day;
- it vomits several times, has a swollen belly, struggles to breathe;
- it can't get up or seems to be in pain;
- it has a seizure or collapses.
