You come home from work and the cushion is in pieces, the door is scratched, the neighbours complain about the barking… Your dog isn't "naughty" or "taking revenge": it may be suffering from **separation anxiety**. The good news: with patience, it gets better.

## How to recognise it

The signs appear **when you leave**, or just after:

- **barking, howling or whining** while you're away;
- **damage near doors and windows** (where you left);
- **peeing or pooping** indoors, although it's normally house-trained;
- it **drools** a lot, pants, paces;
- it **follows you everywhere** at home and gets agitated as soon as you pick up your keys;
- when you come back, it greets you **wildly**, as if you'd been gone for days.

> A puppy that chews everything or a bored dog doesn't necessarily have anxiety. The difference: an anxious dog is **in distress**, and the problems almost always start **within the first minutes** after you leave. Filming your dog with a phone while you're out helps a lot.

## Why does it happen?

- a dog that was **never taught to be alone**, especially one adopted during holidays or a work-from-home period;
- a **change**: moving house, a new job, a new baby, a family member leaving;
- a **rescue dog** that was abandoned before;
- an older dog becoming more dependent.

## What not to do

- **Don't punish it** when you get home. It can't connect the punishment with what it did hours earlier; it only learns to fear your return, and the anxiety grows.
- Don't get a second dog "to keep it company" without advice: it doesn't always solve the problem.
- Don't shut a dog that isn't used to it in a crate: it may hurt itself panicking.

## Steps to help

### 1. Make departures boring
- No big goodbyes or dramatic cuddles before leaving.
- When you return, **ignore it for a few minutes**, then greet it calmly once it has settled.
- Pick up your keys or put on your shoes **without going out**, several times a day: these cues should stop announcing a departure.

### 2. Very short absences, then longer ones
Step out for **30 seconds**, come back. Then 1 minute, 5 minutes, 15 minutes… Increase **only while it stays calm**. If the barking returns, go back to the previous duration. This takes weeks, not days.

### 3. Tire it out before you leave
A **real walk** (not just 5 minutes outside the building) with sniffing and play before you go. A dog that has spent its energy rests more easily.

### 4. Give it something to do
- a **stuffable toy** filled with kibble or food (Kong-style), given **only** when you leave;
- a suitable chew;
- a few kibbles scattered in a snuffle mat.

### 5. A safe spot
A bed in a quiet place, with a piece of clothing that smells of you. Background sound (radio, Quran, soft music) helps some dogs.

### 6. A routine
Dogs like to know what's coming: meals, walks and departures at regular times reassure them.

## When to see the vet

If your dog **hurts itself**, stops eating, or nothing improves after a few weeks, talk to your **vet**. They'll check there's no health problem (pain, urinary issue) and may refer you to a **behaviourist**. In some cases, treatment can help during training.
