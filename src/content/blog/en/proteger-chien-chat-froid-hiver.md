People often think of Morocco as a warm country. Yet between November and March, **nights are cold** in Fès, Meknès, Marrakech or Rabat, and it snows in Ifrane, Azrou and the Atlas. Our dogs and cats feel the cold too. Here's how to protect them.

## Who feels the cold most?

Some animals are more sensitive:

- **puppies and kittens**, who don't regulate their temperature well;
- **older** or sick animals (arthritis, heart or kidney problems);
- **short-haired** or small dogs (chihuahua, pinscher, greyhound…);
- **thin** animals or those rescued from the street.

A husky or a shepherd copes better, but still needs a dry shelter.

## A warm, dry place to sleep

- Keep the bed **away from draughts**, doors and windows.
- **Raise it off the floor**: Moroccan tiles get very cold at night. A thick rug, a blanket or a wooden board is enough.
- If your dog lives outside (terrace, garden), it needs a **kennel closed on three sides**, raised off the ground, with a dry blanket. In heavy cold or rain, bring it inside.
- Change the blanket as soon as it's **wet**: damp bedding chills more than it warms.

## Careful with the kanoun and heaters

> The **kanoun** and charcoal braziers give off **carbon monoxide**, an invisible, odourless gas that is dangerous for you and your pet. Never use them in a closed room.

Other risks:
- **burns**: cats love to curl up against a heater or the kanoun, so add a guard;
- **gas heaters** (butane) in a small room without ventilation;
- heater cables, which puppies love to chew.

## The car engine: a trap for cats

When it's cold, cats (especially street cats) slip **under the bonnet** to enjoy the engine's warmth. Before starting the car in the morning, **knock on the bonnet** or gently sound the horn and wait a few seconds. This small habit saves many cats every winter.

## Antifreeze: sweet and deadly

Coolant (antifreeze) often contains **ethylene glycol**. It tastes sweet and attracts animals, but a few sips can destroy the kidneys. Store containers safely and wipe up leaks in the garage. If in doubt, **go to the vet immediately**, even if your pet seems fine.

## Winter walks

- Walk your dog during the **mildest hours** of the day.
- For small short-haired dogs, a **coat** helps (not for looks: for warmth).
- After rain, **dry it well**, especially the belly and paws.
- In the snow (Ifrane, Michlifen…), check the **paw pads**: ice and salt can damage them. Rinse and dry the paws when you get home.
- **Never leave it alone in a car**: in winter, it gets cold very fast.

## Food and water

- A dog living outdoors burns more energy to stay warm and may need **a little more food**. Ask your vet.
- An indoor dog that moves less, on the other hand, shouldn't eat more.
- In cold regions, make sure outdoor water **doesn't freeze**.

## The coat

Don't shave your dog in winter: its coat is its best protection. **Regular brushing** keeps it clean and insulating. Avoid frequent baths; if you do wash it, dry it completely before going out.

## Signs your pet is too cold

- it **shivers**, curls up, wants to come in;
- it's **weak**, slow, sleepy;
- its ears, paws or tail feel **very cold**;
- it breathes slowly.

Bring it somewhere warm, wrap it in a blanket and **call the vet**. Don't warm it with very hot water or a hair dryer held too close.

## What about street cats?

If you'd like to help the neighbourhood cats: a **cardboard box or plastic crate** with a hole, filled with straw or newspaper (not a blanket, which holds moisture), sheltered from the rain. Add a few kibbles. It's simple and it means a lot.
