Morocco has many abandoned dogs and cats, and many associations looking for families. Adopting means giving an animal a second chance, but it's also a commitment of **10 to 15 years or more**. Here's how to do it well.

## Before adopting: the right questions

Take time to answer honestly:

- **Do I have time?** A dog needs walks every day. A cat needs attention too.
- **Is my home suitable?** Size, balcony, landlord's rules.
- **What budget?** Food, vet, vaccines, neutering, sitting during holidays.
- **Who will look after it when I travel?**
- **Does everyone at home agree?** Including any allergies.

> An animal isn't a gift to give on impulse. Adopt when you're ready, not under pressure.

## Where to adopt in Morocco?

- **Animal welfare associations**: in Casablanca, Rabat, Marrakech, Tangier, Agadir and other cities. They know each animal's personality and often give them vaccinated or neutered.
- **Shelters and refuges**: visit, talk with volunteers.
- **Individuals**: someone who found a kitten, or who can no longer keep their pet.
- **Community apps**: like the Adoption section of DarPaws, where people post animals to adopt **for free**.

Be wary of ads that "give away" animals but ask for a lot of money, or that refuse any meeting.

## Dog or cat, young or adult?

| | Puppy / kitten | Adult |
|---|---|---|
| Education | everything to learn | often already trained |
| Personality | still forming | already known |
| Energy | very high | more stable |
| Adoption | quick | often waiting longer |

Adult and senior animals are adopted less. Yet they're often calm, affectionate and already house-trained.

## Costs to plan for

- **Vet check-up** on arrival;
- **Vaccines** and **deworming**;
- **Neutering** if not done;
- **Equipment**: bowls, bed, lead, carrier, litter tray;
- **Food** every month;
- **Sitting** when you travel.

## The first days at home

1. **Prepare a quiet corner** with a bed, water and, for a cat, a litter tray.
2. **Let it explore at its own pace**. Don't force contact.
3. **Keep a routine**: meals, walks and bedtime at regular times.
4. **Limit visits** in the first days.
5. **Book a vet visit** in the first week.

A cat may hide for several days: that's normal. A dog may have accidents or be anxious when left alone: patience and gentleness.

## Adoption and the law

Responsible adoption also means identifying your pet (microchip if possible), vaccinating it against rabies and not letting it wander. Associations can guide you on local practices.

## Stray animals: how to help without adopting

You can't adopt, but you want to act? There are many ways to help the animals in your neighbourhood:

- **Fostering**: you look after an animal for a few weeks until it finds a permanent family. Associations often provide food and care.
- **Volunteering**: feeding, cleaning shelters, driving animals to the vet.
- **Donations**: food, blankets, or money for care and neutering.
- **Sharing**: spreading adoption posts on your social networks helps enormously.
- **Reporting**: if you see an injured animal, contact a local association.

If you feed cats or dogs in the street, give clean water and suitable food, not spicy leftovers. And find out about **neutering** programmes: it's the most effective long-term way to reduce the number of abandoned animals.

Every small action counts. A shared post can be the one that finds a dog its family.

## FAQ

**Is adoption free?**
On community apps and with many individuals, yes. Associations sometimes ask for a contribution to cover vaccines and neutering.

**Can I adopt if I live in a flat?**
Yes, with the right choice: a cat, or a dog adapted to the space and walked enough.

**What if adoption doesn't work out?**
Contact the association or the previous owner. Never abandon an animal in the street.
